# PulseCare Curated Medical Guidance & Preventive Health

## Cardiovascular Health & Blood Pressure Categories
Maintaining healthy blood pressure is vital for cardiovascular longevity:
- **Normal Blood Pressure**: Systolic less than 120 mm Hg AND diastolic less than 80 mm Hg.
- **Elevated Blood Pressure**: Systolic between 120-129 mm Hg AND diastolic less than 80 mm Hg. Lifestyle modifications recommended.
- **Hypertension Stage 1**: Systolic 130-139 mm Hg OR diastolic 80-89 mm Hg. Medical evaluation and possible ACE inhibitors (like Lisinopril) or lifestyle intervention.
- **Hypertension Stage 2**: Systolic 140 mm Hg or higher OR diastolic 90 mm Hg or higher.
- **Hypertensive Crisis**: Systolic > 180 mm Hg and/or diastolic > 120 mm Hg with symptoms like chest pain or shortness of breath requires immediate emergency room evaluation.

## Type 2 Diabetes Management
- Target Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c) for most non-pregnant adults is typically under 7.0%, customized by a physician.
- First-line oral pharmacological therapy often includes Metformin, which decreases hepatic glucose production and enhances insulin sensitivity.
- Key lifestyle pillars include consistent carbohydrate intake, at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week, and regular foot and retinal exams.

## Dermatology & Skin Cancer Screening (ABCDE Rule)
Regular skin self-examinations help identify suspicious melanomas early:
- **A (Asymmetry)**: One half of the spot does not match the other half.
- **B (Border)**: The edges are irregular, ragged, notched, or blurred.
- **C (Color)**: The color is not uniform; shades of brown, black, pink, red, or white may be present.
- **D (Diameter)**: The spot is larger than 6 millimeters across (about the size of a pencil eraser).
- **E (Evolving)**: The mole is changing in size, shape, color, or symptoms like itching or bleeding.
Any evolving lesion should be evaluated by a dermatologist (such as Dr. Elena Garcia).

## Pediatric Fever Guidance
- Fever is defined clinically as a rectal or oral temperature of 100.4°F (38.0°C) or higher.
- **Urgent Red Flags in Children**: Any fever in infants under 3 months of age requires immediate pediatric medical evaluation. For older children, seek immediate care if fever is accompanied by lethargy, stiff neck, petechial rash, difficulty breathing, or persistent vomiting.
- For mild fevers, ensure adequate hydration and consult your pediatrician (such as Dr. Priya Patel) before administering fever reducers like acetaminophen.
